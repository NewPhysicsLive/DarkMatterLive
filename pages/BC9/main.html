ALP with photon dominance (BC9)

Description of the model

Axion-Like Particles (ALPs) are pseudo-Nambu–Goldstone bosons arising from the spontaneous breaking of a global \(\mathrm{U}(1)\) symmetry at a high scale \(f_a\). Unlike the QCD axion, ALPs do not need to solve the strong CP problem, so their mass \(m_a\) and photon coupling \(g_{a\gamma\gamma}\) are independent free parameters. In the photon-dominance scenario (BC9), the dominant interaction is the two-photon vertex:

\[ \mathcal{L} \;\supset\; -\tfrac{1}{4}\,g_{a\gamma\gamma}\,a\,F_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu} \;=\; g_{a\gamma\gamma}\,a\,\vec{E}\cdot\vec{B}\,, \]

where \(a\) is the ALP field, \(F_{\mu\nu}\) is the electromagnetic field strength, \(\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu} = \tfrac{1}{2}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}F_{\rho\sigma}\) its dual, and \(g_{a\gamma\gamma}\) (units: GeV\(^{-1}\)) is the effective photon coupling. This coupling relates to the Peccei–Quinn scale as \(g_{a\gamma\gamma} = \frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{EM}}}{\pi f_a}\,C_{a\gamma}\), where \(C_{a\gamma}\) is a model-dependent coefficient of order unity.

The two free parameters of the model are \(m_a\) and \(g_{a\gamma\gamma}\), spanning an enormous range: \(m_a \in [10^{-30},\,10^{3}]\,\text{eV}\) and \(g_{a\gamma\gamma} \in [10^{-30},\,1]\,\text{GeV}^{-1}\).

Detection

The photon coupling enables three main detection strategies:

  • Haloscopes (direct detection): resonant microwave cavities or LC circuits placed in a strong static magnetic field convert ambient ALP dark matter into photons via the inverse Primakoff effect. Examples include ADMX, CAPP, HAYSTAC, and QUAX.
  • Helioscopes & light-shining-through-a-wall (laboratory): solar ALPs produced in the Sun's core are reconverted into X-rays in a laboratory magnet (CAST, BabyIAXO, IAXO), or laser photons are converted, propagated through a wall, and reconverted (ALPS‑II, OSQAR, PVLAS).
  • Astrophysical & cosmological observations (indirect detection): ALP–photon oscillations in cosmic or stellar magnetic fields leave imprints such as spectral irregularities in blazar spectra, anomalous stellar cooling, or polarisation signals (Chandra, Fermi, SN 1987A, globular clusters, white dwarfs).

The classification used on this page follows the same convention as the rest of the site:

By detection type:

  • Direct detection: haloscopes, helioscopes, and light-shining-through-a-wall experiments;
  • Indirect detection: astrophysical observations and cosmological measurements.

By experiment type:

  • Laboratory experiments: haloscopes, helioscopes, LSW experiments, magnetometers;
  • Astrophysical observations: stellar cooling (globular clusters, white dwarfs, neutron stars), SN 1987A, X-ray telescopes;
  • Cosmological measurements: CMB spectral distortions, BBN, extragalactic background light;
  • Collider experiments: LHC heavy-ion runs, BaBar, beam-dump searches.

By time frame:

  • Past constraints: results published before 2015;
  • Recent constraints: 2015 to present;
  • Planned/future constraints: projected sensitivities of proposed experiments (e.g., IAXO, DMRadio, AION).

By assumption:

  • None\(^*\): model-independent constraints (laboratory and collider searches);
  • Dark Matter: constraints that assume ALPs constitute all or part of the observed dark matter density.

\(^*\) As with all models, simplifying assumptions are always present; "None" here means the constraint does not require ALPs to make up the dark matter.
